French

Looking for the best YouTube channels to learn French? Start with Easy French. It’s free, authentic, and works from B1 upward.

But the right channel for you depends entirely on where you are and what you’re trying to fix.

This list covers 22 free French YouTube channels organized by level and goal, from animated beginner explainers to 24-hour news streams for advanced learners. Whether you’re starting from zero or preparing for DALF C2, there’s something here for every stage of the journey.

🟢 For Beginners (A1–B1)

1. 1 Jour 1 Question

Best for: Absolute beginners, cultural context, classroom-style learning

1 Jour 1 Question (“One Day, One Question”) is one of the most beginner-friendly French channels on YouTube. Each short episode answers a single question about French culture, history, or society — in simple, clear language with matching animations that help you follow along even when your vocabulary is still limited.

The format is naturally comprehensible: you always know what the topic is before you press play, which gives your brain a huge head start. It’s also great for building cultural knowledge that comes up in DELF exam topics.

Pro tip: Pause after each segment and try to summarize what you heard in French — even one or two sentences. This turns passive watching into active practice.

Level: A1–B1

2. frenchsounds

Best for: Pronunciation, phonetics, getting your accent right from the start

If you’ve ever felt like French pronunciation is a mystery — why do half the letters seem to be silent? — frenchsounds is the channel you need.

This phonetics specialist breaks down French sounds in detail: exactly where to place your tongue, how to shape your lips, and why French words flow the way they do. The explanations are methodical and patient, the kind of teaching that takes something intimidating and makes it genuinely learnable.

Don’t skip pronunciation work early on. Bad habits formed in the first few months are the hardest to fix later.

Level: A1–B1

3. Just Speak French

Best for: Beginners to intermediate, podcast-style listening, bilingual subtitles

Just Speak French is a teaching-focused podcast channel designed specifically for learners — similar in spirit to the popular innerFrench channel, but with two key differences: episodes are shorter (easier to fit into a busy day), and videos come with both French and Chinese subtitles, making it especially useful for Mandarin speakers learning French.

The pace is controlled and deliberate without feeling artificial. A good entry point for training your ear before tackling authentic native-speed content.

Level: A2–B1

🔵 For Intermediate Learners (B1–B2)

4. Easy French

Best for: Natural spoken French, colloquial vocabulary, accent exposure

Easy French sends a host into the streets of Paris to ask locals about whatever’s on people’s minds — relationships, social media, money, daily life. The responses are unscripted, fast, and completely real.

This is where you close the gap between the French you learned in class and the French people actually speak. You’ll quickly pick up filler words, contractions native speakers use constantly, and the rhythm of natural conversation. Most videos include English subtitles as a safety net, but try watching without them first.

Street interviews like these are ideal material for shadowing practice — unscripted, natural pace, real connected speech.

Level: B1–B2

5. Un Gars Une Fille

Best for: Conversational French, couple dynamics, comic timing

A classic of French-language television, Un Gars Une Fille follows a couple — Chouchou and Loulou — through hilariously relatable everyday situations. They bicker, misunderstand each other, and somehow stay together anyway. Jean Dujardin (yes, The Artist Oscar winner) plays the male lead, and his comedic instincts make every scene land.

The dialogue is fast and natural, but because scenes are short and situations are familiar, context carries you through. Great for picking up how couples actually talk to each other in French — including all the interruptions and overlapping speech that textbooks never show you.

Level: B1–B2

6. VeryFrenchTrip

Best for: Travel content, fast conversational French, listening endurance

Sixtine and Inès are two French women who travel across Asia — South Korea, Japan, China, Thailand — and bring their unfiltered energy to every video. They talk a lot. They talk fast. They talk over each other. It’s chaotic and completely authentic.

For intermediate learners, this is a great endurance test: can you keep up when native speakers are genuinely excited about something? The travel content also means you get vocabulary across a huge range of topics and contexts.

Level: B1–B2

7. Le Journal d’Olympe

Best for: Lifestyle French, natural vlogging style, personality-driven content

Olympe is a French creator who built her following through candid, thoughtful content about dissociative identity disorder — content that was both educational and genuinely moving. She’s since shifted toward everyday life vlogs, but her distinctive voice and personality remain.

What makes her channel useful for learners is that she speaks naturally and expressively — the kind of French you’d hear from a thoughtful, articulate young Parisian. Following a creator you genuinely like over months or years is one of the most underrated immersion strategies.

Search YouTube for “Le Journal d’Olympe” to find her channel.

Level: B1–B2

8. Benoit Le Corre

Best for: Documentary-style French, human interest stories, real-world vocabulary

Benoit is a young French journalist who follows and profiles ordinary people living extraordinary or unconventional lives. The production quality is high, the subjects are genuinely interesting, and every video comes with a thoughtfully written description.

This channel sits in that sweet spot between documentary and personal essay — culturally rich, never boring, and a great source of real-world vocabulary across many different social contexts.

Level: B2

9. Vanity Fair France

Best for: Entertainment French, celebrity interviews, pop culture vocabulary

The official YouTube channel of Vanity Fair France uploads interviews, behind-the-scenes clips, and features with French and international celebrities. The format is relaxed and conversational — less scripted than press junkets, more like genuine exchanges — which makes it surprisingly natural listening material.

If you’re a film fan, a music lover, or just someone who actually enjoys the content you’re studying with, this is an easy channel to stick with long-term. Engagement is the most underrated factor in language learning consistency.

Level: B1–B2

10. TEDx Talks (French Playlist)

Best for: All levels, accent variety, interest-based learning

TEDx has hundreds of French-language talks organized by language in the Playlists section. Unlike TED mainstage talks, TEDx events are more conversational and community-focused — often easier to follow as a result.

Filter by French and pick topics you already know something about. Familiar subject matter reduces the cognitive load, which lets you focus on the language itself rather than scrambling to understand the content.

Level: All levels

🔴 For Advanced Learners (C1–C2)

11. Arte

Best for: DELF B2–DALF C2 exam prep, structured argumentation, topic knowledge

Arte is a Franco-German public broadcaster whose YouTube channel consistently delivers high-quality content on humanities, science, history, and social issues. Each video follows a clear structure: introduce a topic, present multiple perspectives, support with data. Sound familiar? That’s almost exactly the format required for DALF written production and oral expression tasks.

Long-term strategy: Pick one topic area (environment, technology, society) and watch 3–4 Arte videos on it before your next speaking practice. You’ll never be stuck for things to say.

Also worth exploring: 28 Minutes is Arte’s flagship debate programme — a fast-paced panel discussion covering culture, politics, and current events. Where Arte’s documentary content gives you structured argument, 28 Minutes throws you into live discussion with multiple speakers talking over each other. Significantly harder, but excellent C2-level listening practice.

Level: B2–C2

12. Luqei

Best for: DALF C1–C2 strategy, sophisticated expression, exam-specific content

Good C2 resources are genuinely rare. By that level, most learners are expected to source their own materials — but knowing how to approach the C2 exam is a different skill entirely.

Luqei fills this gap: DALF exam strategy in detail, with most content focused on C2 specifically — how the exam is scored, what examiners look for, and how to elevate your French from competent to genuinely impressive. The vocabulary and phrasing tips are the kind you won’t find in any textbook.

Level: C1–C2

13. Palmashow

Best for: Comedy fluency, cultural literacy, fast-speech training

Two French comedians. Everyday situations. Exaggerated to the point of absurdity. Extremely fast delivery.

Palmashow serves as a benchmark: when you start genuinely finding it funny rather than just following the plot, your French has reached a new level. The humor is rooted in specifically French cultural moments — workplace dynamics, social awkwardness, French bureaucracy — so understanding the jokes means you’ve actually internalized something about how French people see their world.

Level: C1–C2

14. Montreux Comedy

Best for: Stand-up French, regional variety, francophone comedy

The official channel of the Montreux Comedy Festival in Switzerland, Montreux Comedy uploads clips from French-speaking comedians across the francophone world — France, Belgium, Switzerland, North Africa, and beyond. Clips are typically around 10 minutes: the perfect length for intensive listening practice.

Stand-up is linguistically rich — comedians play with language, use wordplay, reference current events, and speak at performance pace. A genuine challenge even at C1 level.

Level: C1–C2

15. Learn French with Vincent

Best for: Grammar, systematic reference, filling gaps at any level

Vincent’s channel is the closest thing to a complete French grammar encyclopedia on YouTube — A1 through C2, all explained clearly and methodically. Whether you’re puzzling over the subjunctive, struggling with verb agreement, or trying to understand when to use imparfait vs passé composé, there’s almost certainly a video for it.

Use it as a primary resource at the beginning, or dip back in to fix specific gaps at any point in your learning.

Level: A1–C2

16. Comme une Française

Best for: Cultural fluency, idiomatic French, the layer underneath the language

Comme une Française focuses on something most language courses ignore entirely: the cultural logic underneath the language. Host Géraldine explains not just what French people say, but why — and what it reveals about French culture.

Why do French people say Merci bien more naturally than Merci beaucoup? What does Je suis crevé actually convey that Je suis fatigué doesn’t? Understanding these nuances is what takes your French from technically correct to genuinely natural.

Level: B1–C1

17. French Mornings with Elisa

Best for: Professional French, workplace communication, business emails

Business French is its own register — and it’s one that most general learning channels don’t cover well. French Mornings with Elisa does.

Elisa tackles the specific situations that come up in professional French contexts: how to open a formal email, how to politely interrupt in a meeting, how to strike the right tone in a negotiation. These aren’t abstract vocabulary lists — they’re practical scripts for real professional moments.

Level: B1–C1

18. HelloFrench — Learn French with Elisabeth

Best for: Comprehensive learning, structured content, bilingual subtitles

With 200+ videos covering grammar, vocabulary, current events, and everyday conversation, HelloFrench is one of the most complete French learning channels on YouTube. Every video comes with French subtitles built in, with additional subtitle options in other languages — a rare and genuinely useful feature for learners who want to read along without hunting for transcripts elsewhere.

Level: A1–C1

📰 For News & Passive Listening (C1+)

19. franceinfo

Best for: Advanced learners, passive immersion, real-time French news

franceinfo is a 24-hour rolling news channel — the kind you can put on in the background while you work. That’s one of the most underrated ways to use it: not intensive study, but low-pressure exposure that keeps French in your environment throughout the day.

The key is being honest about what you’re doing. Background listening and focused listening are two different activities with different goals. If franceinfo is on while you’re doing something else, treat it as ambient immersion — don’t expect it to replace dedicated study sessions. The vocabulary skews toward politics, economics, and current affairs, so this channel works best at C1 and above.

Level: C1–C2

20. France24

Best for: International news in French, advanced listening, geopolitical vocabulary

France24 covers international news from a French perspective — geopolitics, global economics, foreign affairs. Where franceinfo leans toward domestic French news, France24 takes a broader international view, which means more varied vocabulary and a wider range of topics.

Both channels work well as a pair. If you’re building the topic knowledge that DALF C1 and C2 oral and written tasks demand, having either of these on regularly is one of the more painless ways to do it.

Level: C1–C2

🔬 For Language Enthusiasts

21. Linguisticae

Best for: Linguistics nerds, advanced learners, anyone curious about how language works

Linguisticae is less a French learning channel and more a French-language channel about language. The host digs into how languages work, evolve, and interact with culture — and he’s genuinely good at making academic concepts feel exciting.

Coverage leans toward European languages, but he regularly touches on Chinese, Japanese, and other non-European systems too. Episodes explore language in AI, the linguistics of Star Wars worldbuilding, vocabulary in online gaming culture, and other topics you’d never expect to find in a linguistics series. Your French needs to be solid to follow comfortably.

Level: B2–C2

🌱 One to Watch

22. Gouzou Bros

Best for: Early followers, Franco-Chinese cultural content

Still in its earliest days, Gouzou Bros is the new joint project of a French-Chinese creator and her brother, exploring the space between French and Chinese culture. Only a handful of videos so far — worth following early to see where it goes.

Quick Reference: All 22 Channels

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best YouTube channel to learn French for free? All 22 channels on this list are completely free. For most learners, Easy French is the best starting point at intermediate level. Absolute beginners should start with 1 Jour 1 Question or Learn French with Vincent.

Which French YouTube channel is best for absolute beginners? 1 Jour 1 Question, frenchsounds, and Just Speak French are the most beginner-friendly options on this list.

Can I reach B2 French just by watching YouTube? YouTube alone won’t get you there, but it’s an excellent complement to structured study. Channels like Arte and Easy French provide authentic input that textbooks can’t replicate. Pair consistent watching with active practice and you’ll find YouTube becomes a genuinely powerful part of the process.

What French YouTube channels are best for DALF prep? Arte and Luqei are the two most targeted options. Arte builds the topic knowledge and argumentation skills tested in written and oral production. Luqei covers exam strategy specifically at C1 and C2 level.

Are news channels like franceinfo good for learning French? At C1 and above, yes — as passive immersion or active listening practice. Below that level, news French tends to be too dense and fast-paced to be useful. Build your listening foundation first, then add news channels when you’re ready.

Final Thoughts

The best French YouTube channel is the one you’ll actually keep watching. Start where your level is, bookmark one or two channels that feel slightly out of reach, and let your curiosity guide which topics you follow.

All 22 channels here are free. The hard part isn’t access — it’s building the habit.

Bonne chance — et bonne écoute ! 🇫🇷
